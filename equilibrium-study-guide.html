01

Core Concepts — Energy & Equilibrium

Before tackling Le Chatelier, you need to be fluent in two things: which direction absorbs heat, and what equilibrium actually means.

Endothermic vs. Exothermic — treat heat like a chemical

The cleanest way to handle thermochemistry in equilibrium problems is to write "Heat" right into the equation. That converts every temperature question into a concentration question.

🔵 Endothermic

Absorbs heat from surroundings (feels cold).

Heat + A + B ⇌ C + D

Heat is on the reactant side. Going forward (→) uses up heat — that's the absorption.

Examples: melting ice, photosynthesis, cold packs (NH₄NO₃ dissolving).

🔴 Exothermic

Releases heat to surroundings (feels warm).

A + B ⇌ C + D + Heat

Heat is on the product side. Going forward (→) releases heat.

Examples: combustion, hand warmers, neutralization, respiration.

⚡ Memory Hook

ENdothermic = ENergy IN. Heat is a reactant.

EXothermic = EXits as energy. Heat is a product.

What is "equilibrium"?

Equilibrium is reached when the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal. The concentrations of reactants and products no longer change — even though molecules are still reacting in both directions. It's a dynamic balance, not a frozen one.

🎯 Big Idea Connecting Q1–Q4

Every Le Chatelier question reduces to one move: identify the stress, then shift the equilibrium AWAY from it. If you added it, the system consumes it. If you removed it, the system replaces it.

02

Le Chatelier's Principle — The Universal Rule

"When a system at equilibrium is disturbed by a change in conditions, the equilibrium shifts in the direction that counteracts the disturbance."

The Le Chatelier Decision Tree
Identify the STRESS
↓
Added something?
Removed something?
↓
Shift AWAY from it
(consume the addition)
Shift TOWARD it
(replace the loss)
↓
Which side is it on? That tells you direction.

💡 Pro Tip — Why Volume/Pressure Matters

For gas-phase reactions, decreasing volume (= increasing pressure) shifts equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas. Increasing volume shifts toward more moles. Volume changes don't affect equilibria where both sides have equal moles of gas, or pure liquid/solid systems.

The three factors that shift equilibrium

FactorWhat changesHow equilibrium responds
Concentration Add/remove a reactant or product Shifts away from added, toward removed
Temperature Heat added or removed (treat as a chemical) Shifts away from heat addition, toward heat removal
Volume / Pressure Compress or expand a gas system Shifts toward side with fewer (↑P) or more (↓P) moles of gas
⚠️ What does NOT shift equilibrium

Adding a catalyst speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally — equilibrium is reached faster, but the position does not change.

03

Walking Through the Four Questions

Each question is answered with the reasoning that connects it back to the core concept. Try to answer before revealing.

Question 1
Endothermic Direction
For an endothermic reaction, energy is absorbed when the reaction runs in _______.
  • ✓ A. The forward direction
  • ✗ B. The reverse direction
  • ✗ C. Neither direction
  • ✗ D. Either direction
Why "forward"?

Write the endothermic reaction as Heat + A + B ⇌ C + D. The forward reaction (left → right) converts heat into chemical bonds — heat is consumed, i.e., absorbed. The reverse direction would release heat. So energy absorption only happens going forward.

Question 2
Adding a Reactant
Adding reactant to an equilibrium system will cause the reaction to move in a _______ direction to _______ the added reactant.
  • ✓ A. Forward; consume
  • ✗ B. Reverse; consume
  • ✗ C. Forward; build
  • ✗ D. Reverse; build
Why "forward; consume"?

Le Chatelier says the system shifts away from the stress. Adding reactant = extra "stuff" on the left side. To relieve that stress, the reaction proceeds forward (→) to consume the excess and produce more product. "Build" is a distractor — the reaction never creates more of what was just added; it uses it up.

Question 3
Predict the Shift
According to Le Chatelier's Principle, predict the equilibrium shift for: A + B ⇌ C + D + Heat. Which statement is correct?
  • ✓ A. Decreasing A shifts the reaction to the left
  • ✗ B. Decreasing B shifts reaction to the right
  • ✗ C. Increasing temperature shifts the reaction to the right
  • ✗ D. Increasing D shifts the reaction to the right
Why "decreasing A shifts left"?

This is exothermic (Heat is on the product side). Walk through each option:

• A ✓ — Removing reactant A leaves the system "short" on the left. It shifts left (reverse) to replace A. Correct.

• B ✗ — Removing B also shifts left (same logic), not right.

• C ✗ — Heat is a product. Adding heat shifts away from heat → left, not right.

• D ✗ — Adding product D shifts away from D → left, not right.

Question 4
Factors Affecting Equilibrium
According to Le Chatelier's Principle, factors that determine the equilibrium conditions of a system are:
  • ✗ A. Temperature
  • ✗ B. Concentration
  • ✗ C. Volume
  • ✓ D. All of the above
Why "all of the above"?

All three are valid stresses. Temperature shifts equilibrium because heat acts like a chemical in the reaction. Concentration shifts it because adding/removing reactant or product creates an imbalance. Volume (for gases) shifts it because compressing a gas changes effective concentration and favors the side with fewer moles. A catalyst is the famous exception — it does not shift equilibrium.

🎓 Pattern across all four questions

Every question is testing the same core skill: identify the stress, then apply "away from added, toward removed." The only twist is whether the stress is concentration, temperature (treat heat as a chemical), or volume. Memorize that one rule and you've got the whole topic.

Endothermic vs. Exothermic — At a Glance

Endothermic

Heat + A + B ⇌ C + D

• Absorbs heat going forward
• Feels cold to touch
• ΔH > 0 (positive)
• Heat = REACTANT

Exothermic

A + B ⇌ C + D + Heat

• Releases heat going forward
• Feels warm to touch
• ΔH < 0 (negative)
• Heat = PRODUCT

🔑 The One Rule You Need

"Equilibrium shifts away from what you add and toward what you remove."

📝 Common Trap Words

"Build" or "create" the added substance → wrong. The system consumes what you add, never builds more of it.

"Increasing temperature always shifts forward" → wrong. It depends on whether the reaction is endo- or exothermic. Always identify which side heat is on.
